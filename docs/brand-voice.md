# Mind Clear — Brand Voice Guide

This document defines how Mind Clear sounds across every touchpoint: website, app store, social media, email, blog, and ads. Read this before writing any copy.

---

## Voice in Three Words

**Direct. Warm. Real.**

We sound like a calm, understanding friend — someone who has ADHD themselves and knows what a bad brain day feels like. We don't lecture. We don't motivate. We just help.

---

## Tone Spectrum

Mind Clear lives in a specific emotional range. Here's where we sit:

- **Encouraging** — not motivational. We don't pump people up. We gently say "you've got this" without the exclamation marks.
- **Honest** — not blunt. We acknowledge hard things ("some days are just hard") without being dramatic about it.
- **Simple** — not dumbed down. Short sentences because clarity matters, not because we think the reader can't handle complexity.
- **Conversational** — not casual. We're friendly but not slangy. We don't try to sound young or trendy.
- **Confident** — not salesy. We believe the product helps. We don't need to convince with hype.

---

## Writing Rules

### Sentence structure
- Short sentences. If a sentence has a comma and an "and," it's probably two sentences.
- Lead with the important thing. Don't bury it.
- One idea per paragraph in marketing copy.

### Word choice
- Use "you" and "your" — speak directly to the reader.
- Use plain words. "Start" not "commence." "Help" not "empower." "Clear" not "declutter."
- Name the feeling: "overwhelmed," "stuck," "scattered," "paralyzed." These are words our audience uses about themselves.

### What to avoid
- **Corporate jargon:** leverage, synergy, optimize, streamline, unlock, revolutionize, cutting-edge, game-changer, next-level, best-in-class
- **AI hype:** revolutionary AI, powered by advanced AI, intelligent assistant, smart AI, AI-driven insights
- **Wellness-speak:** self-care journey, mindfulness practice, holistic approach, optimize your wellness
- **Pressure language:** Don't miss out, limited time, act now, you're falling behind, stop wasting time
- **Filler phrases:** "It's important to note that," "In today's fast-paced world," "At the end of the day," "When it comes to"
- **Passive voice** (when active works): "Tasks are organized by the AI" → "The AI organizes your tasks"

## Voice by Channel

### Website
Most polished version of the voice. Still warm and direct, but crafted. Every word earns its place. Headlines are punchy. Body copy is clear and scannable.

**Example:**
> Your head is full. Your to-do list is longer than your day. And you don't know where to start.
>
> Mind Clear does. Dump everything on your mind — the AI sorts it, prioritizes it, and turns it into a plan you can actually follow.

### App Store
Slightly more structured (metadata constraints), but same voice. Feature-focused but always tied back to the feeling.

**Example:**
> Dump your thoughts. Get a plan. Mind Clear's AI-powered Brain Dump takes everything swirling in your head and turns it into organized, prioritized goals and tasks — in minutes.

### Social Media
Looser, more personal. Brian's voice comes through more directly. Humor is welcome (especially self-deprecating ADHD humor). Emojis are fine but not required.

**Example (TikTok/Instagram):**
> me: I'll just write everything down and organize it later
> also me: *stares at list for 40 minutes*
> 
> Brain Dump does the organizing part for you. link in bio.

### Email
Warm, like writing to one person. No corporate email voice. Subject lines are conversational, not clickbait.

**Example subject line:** "Your brain dump is waiting"
**Example opening:** "Hey — quick one. If your head feels full today, open the app and do a Brain Dump. That's it. Just get it all out. The AI handles the rest."

### Blog / Content
Most room to breathe. Longer form, but still direct. First-person (Brian) or second-person (to the reader). Real examples, real talk. Not academic, not fluffy.

**Example opening:**
> I've tried every productivity app. Todoist, Notion, Things, Habitica, TickTick — you name it. And every single one assumed I could already organize my thoughts enough to use them. That's the problem nobody talks about.

## Do / Don't Quick Reference

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "Clear your head" | "Optimize your workflow" |
| "Built for ADHD brains" | "Designed for neurodiverse individuals" |
| "When you're stuck, it helps" | "AI-powered task decomposition engine" |
| "Missing a day is fine" | "Maintain your streak to build momentum" |
| "One task. Right now." | "Prioritize your most impactful deliverable" |
| "Dump everything on your mind" | "Input your tasks and let our AI categorize them" |
| "The app handles it" | "Leverage our intelligent automation" |
| "Some days are just hard" | "Everyone has off days on their productivity journey" |

## The Founder Voice

Brian is the face of Mind Clear. When content comes from him directly:

- First person. "I built this because..."
- Honest about ADHD. Not performative about it.
- Shows the messy parts of building. Not just wins.
- Never positions himself as a guru or thought leader. He's a dad with ADHD who built the app he needed.
